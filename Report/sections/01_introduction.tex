% General
Tropical forests are one of the world's key ecosystems, providing habitat for a wide diversity of species, supporting local livelihoods, and playing an integral role in global ecological cycles such as carbon sequestration, water regulation, and nutrient cycling \citep{bormaCarbonContributionsSouth2022}. They host approximately two-thirds of the world's flora and fauna, making them essential habitats for global biodiversity \citep{mulatuBiodiversityMonitoringChanging2017}. At the same time, they support local livelihoods of millions of people who rely on their resources for food, medicine, and income \citep{bormaCarbonContributionsSouth2022}. Furthermore, approximately 30\% of all CO2 emitted by human activities are estimated to be removed from the atmosphere by tropical forests, positioning them as a critical component in the global climate regulation \citep{bormaCarbonContributionsSouth2022}. 

Despite their importance, over half of the tropical forests have been deforested and converted into other land use types, with agriculture being the most common, resulting in widespread loss of these vital ecosystem services \citep{chazdonNaturalRegenerationTool2016, arroyo-rodriguezMultipleSuccessionalPathways2017}. In the tropics, shifting agriculture is a common practice that, once natural resources are depleted, often leads to land abandonment \citep{hordijkLandUseHistory2024}. Abandoned lands, in turn, frequently undergo natural regrowth. Given the growing pressure on tropical forests, natural forest regeneration represents a promising solution for ecological restoration \citep{hordijkLandUseHistory2024}. During this natural recovery process tree species colonize land following human-induced or natural disturbances \citep{crouzeillesEcologicalRestorationSuccess2017}. Over time, vegetation and soil can recover, resulting in the formation of a secondary forest \citep{chazdonSecondGrowthPromise2014}. However, the success of natural regeneration is strongly influenced by seed inflow, which relies on the availability of seed sources and dispersers in the surrounding forest landscape. Seed inflow is therefore dependent on the spatial factors, such as the degree of fragmentation and the proximity to surrounding forest \citep{arroyo-rodriguezMultipleSuccessionalPathways2017}. Although substantial research has been published on the importance of surrounding forest landscape during natural forest regeneration \citep{arroyo-rodriguezMultipleSuccessionalPathways2017, v.h.safarLandscapeOpennessHas2022a}, there is an existing knowledge gap on how the surrounding forest impacts the woody plant seed properties in early secondary forests and how this varies between wet and dry tropical forest. Therefore, this study aims to investigate the effects that the surrounding forest landscape has on woody plant seed rain properties, and compare these effects between wet and dry tropical secondary forests.

The underlying ecological mechanism of natural forest recovery is secondary succession, which is a directional change in ecosystem attributes over time following a disturbance. Secondary succession is a process during which previously vegetated land recovers after a disturbance, beginning with the colonization of herbaceous plants and gradually transitioning to woody species. Unlike primary succession, secondary succession can benefit from land legacies such as well-established soils or the presence of propagules, the reproductive units of plants \citep{poorterSuccessionalTheories2023}. % here a potential to elaborate on the history of succession theories development
Successional theory has evolved over time, with 
key frameworks proposed by \citet{pickettHierarchicalConsiderationCauses1987} and \citet{poorterComprehensiveFrameworkVegetation2024}, identifying three main successional mechanisms: (i) site availability, (ii) species availability, and (iii) species performance. Site availability depends on the type, intensity and duration of a disturbance, which in turn impacts seed bank and remnant vegetation \citep{poorterComprehensiveFrameworkVegetation2024}. Species availability is regulated by propagules that remain at the site after the disturbance and those dispersed into the site \citep{gleasonIndividualisticConceptPlant1926, dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}. Finally, species performance is determined by species traits, biotic interactions, and the abiotic environment \citep{poorterComprehensiveFrameworkVegetation2024}. 

% On seeds
Among these, species availability often has the strongest influence on the speed and the direction of the first phase of succession \citep{poorterSuccessionalTheories2023, dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}. However, due to the land history of common slash and burn agriculture in the tropics, remnant propagules such as seedbanks and resprouts might be limited or depleted \citep{bezerraDrasticImpoverishmentSoil2022}. Thus, leaving the species availability mechanism dependent on propagules dispersed into the site, also known as seed rain. The diversity and abundance of seeds in the seed rain are thereafter important factors for secondary succession. They are dependent on and classified by the agent they are dispersed by; a biotic disperser such as birds and mammals or an abiotic disperser such as wind and water \citep{poorterComprehensiveFrameworkVegetation2024}. Additionally, seeds can be also classified according to the functional guild the species belong to. These are pioneer species, rapid colonizers benefiting from open and disturbed landscapes \citep{poorterComprehensiveFrameworkVegetation2024}; or shade-tolerant species, benefiting from low light conditions typically found in established forest communities \citep{dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}; and generalist species, able to thrive on various resources and often found in disturbed landscapes. Though, the mode of seed dispersal, the functional guild, and therefore the diversity of seeds in the seed rain is, in turn, dependent on the forest landscape surrounding the regenerating forest site.

Early successional stages are most commonly characterized by wind, birds and bats dispersal, whereas later successional stages, when seed abundance increases are more commonly characterized by larger mammals dispersal \citep{dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}. Moreover, dispersal mode can also vary between wet and dry tropical forest. In dry tropical forest, seeds are more likely to be smaller and dispersed by wind, whereas in wet tropical forest, seeds tend to be covered in fleshy fruit and dispersed by frugivores \citep{vieiraPrinciplesNaturalRegeneration2006, dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}. The mode of seed dispersal and therefore the abundance and diversity of seeds is, however, in turn dependent on the landscape surrounding the regenerating forest site.

Moreover, the surrounding forest functions as a seed source and as a habitat for seed dispersers and pollinators \citep{hordijkLandUseHistory2024, arroyo-rodriguezMultipleSuccessionalPathways2017}. Older forests tend to have higher diversity and richness of seeds and thus having the potential to provide higher quantity and variety of seeds to the regenerating forest sites \citep{poorterSuccessionalTheories2023}. Similarly to forest age, larger forest cover tends to represent higher seed abundance and hence positively impacting the seed inflow to the regenerating site \citep{huancanunezSeedrainsuccessionalFeedbacksWet2021}. Furthermore, it is not only the presence of surrounding forest, but also the degree of connectivity between the existent forest patches that has a strong influence on the regenerating forest site. Well connected forest patches positively impact abundance of animal dispersed seeds, whereas more distanced forest patches have usually higher abundance of wind dispersed seeds \citep{dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}. However, given that most seeds are distributed over short distances, sites in close proximity to an established forest tend to display higher seed abundance \citep{chazdonSecondGrowthPromise2014, hordijkLandUseHistory2024}.

The forest landscape factors such as forest cover, forest connectivity, and forest successional stage play a critical role in the seed abundance, diversity and distribution \citep{poorterComprehensiveFrameworkVegetation2024, i.hordijkLandUseHistory2024, v.h.safarLandscapeOpennessHas2022a}. Moreover, the surrounding forest functions as a seed source and as a habitat for seed dispersers and pollinators \citep{hordijkLandUseHistory2024, arroyo-rodriguezMultipleSuccessionalPathways2017}. Forests with larger cover tend to have a higher abundance and diversity of seeds and thus having the potential to provide more seeds to the regenerating forest sites \citep{poorterSuccessionalTheories2023}. Furthermore, the abundance and diversity of animal dispersed seeds in the seed rain of a regenerating forest patch tend to be higher when the patch is well connected to surrounding forest areas as it provides a potential for animal movement \citep{v.h.safarLandscapeOpennessHas2022a}. On the other hand, less connected regenerating forest patch to the surrounding forest areas tends to have higher abundance and diversity of wind dispersed seeds due to the more open landscape favoring abiotic dispersal \citep{dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}. However, given that most seeds are distributed over short distances, sites in close proximity to an established forest tend to display an overall higher seed abundance \citep{chazdonSecondGrowthPromise2014, hordijkLandUseHistory2024}. Similarly to forest cover, older forest tends to support higher seed abundance and diversity due to higher number of reproductive trees, developed habitat structure and thereof higher presence of shade-tolerant species. Hence, presence of older forest in the surrounding landscape positively impacts the seed inflow to the regenerating site \citep{huancanunezSeedrainsuccessionalFeedbacksWet2021}.

However, the dominant mode of dispersal might vary during different stages of succession. Early successional stages are most commonly characterized by wind, birds and bats dispersal, whereas later successional stages, when seed abundance increases are more commonly characterized by larger mammals dispersal \citep{dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}. This is due to the presence of more open landscape at the beginning of succession which facilitates abiotic dispersal and later development of more complex vegetation facilitates, in turn, biotic dispersal. Moreover, dispersal mode can also vary between wet and dry tropical forest. In dry tropical forest, seeds are more likely to be smaller and dispersed by wind, whereas in wet tropical forest, seeds tend to be covered in fleshy fruit and dispersed by frugivores \citep{vieiraPrinciplesNaturalRegeneration2006, dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021}. Furthermore, unlike in dry tropical forest, succession in wet tropical forest is more dependent on the forest landscape connectivity and the availability of seed dispersers, as most tree species rely on animals for dispersal \citep{hordijkLandUseHistory2024, lohbeckSuccessionalChangesFunctional2013}.

To study these ecological dynamics effectively, it is essential to accurately capture changes in the surrounding forest landscape. Remote sensing using historical records plays a pivotal role in quantifying and understanding forest dynamics. As a time series algorithm, \ac{AVOCADO} analyzes more than two satellite images to detect a change. Compared to bi-temporal difference or supervised image classification methods, time series approaches offer greater precision in detecting small-scale forest changes by capturing the dynamic behavior of vegetation over time. This advantage is essential when analyzing forest change dynamics within a smallholder agricultural landscape, which is often characterized by complex vegetation dynamics caused by forest degradation and shifting cultivation \citep{jakovacRoleLanduseHistory2021}. Unlike many traditional time series approaches, AVOCADO can detect multiple disturbance and regrowth events while adapting to site-specific phenological variability \citep{decuyperContinuousMonitoringForest2022}. This capacity is particularly relevant when assessing how forest landscape dynamics influence ecological processes during forest regeneration.

Despite the substantial research published on the mechanisms of secondary forest succession related to forest landscape factors \citep{arroyo-rodriguezLandscapescaleForestCover2023a, i.hordijkLandUseHistory2024, v.h.safarLandscapeOpennessHas2022a, martinez-ramosNaturalForestRegeneration2016}, few studies have investigated the detailed effects of their impact on woody plant seed diversity and community properties and compared them between early successional dry and wet tropical forest ecosystems. Understanding the mechanisms of secondary succession broadens our knowledge on how ecosystems respond to a disturbance and how they can potentially recover. This is particularly important in the Anthropocene during which global land use and climate change cause severe disruptions in forest ecosystems \citep{poorterSuccessionalTheories2023}.  Therefore, the aim of this study is to understand how forest landscape factors affect seed diversity, dispersal mode and the functional guild of species in a dry and wet tropical forest during early succession.

\subsection{Hypotheses}

Seed richness was hypothesized to increase with a higher surrounding forest cover, consistent with the "habitat amount hypothesis," which predicts that larger habitat areas support more species due to higher habitat availability \citep{arroyo-rodriguezLandscapescaleForestCover2023a, PEREZCARDENAS2021118818, arroyo-rodriguezMultipleSuccessionalPathways2017, fahrig2013rethinking}. Similarly, higher forest connectivity and a greater proportion of late successional forest in the landscape were predicted to enhance seed richness by facilitating dispersal and colonization, and by providing a source of diverse propagules \citep{huancanunezSeedrainsuccessionalFeedbacksWet2021, hordijkLandUseHistory2024, v.h.safarLandscapeOpennessHas2022a, martinez-ramosNaturalForestRegeneration2016, goosemForestAgeIsolation2016}. These effects were hypothesized to occur in both dry and wet tropical forest types.

Regarding seed dispersal modes, we predicted that the proportion of biotically dispersed seeds would increase with forest cover, connectivity, and late successional forest, reflecting the reliance of animal dispersers on larger, more connected, and mature forest patches \citep{chazdonDriversBenefitsNatural2025, martinez-ramosNaturalForestRegeneration2016, hordijkLandUseHistory2024, v.h.safarLandscapeOpennessHas2022a}. In contrast, abiotically dispersed seeds were expected to decline with increasing forest cover and connectivity, as dense and connected forests reduce wind exposure and limit dispersal distances \citep{poorterComprehensiveFrameworkVegetation2024, damschen2014fragmentation}.

Pioneer and generalist species were predicted to decrease with increasing forest cover, connectivity, and late successional forest, reflecting their adaptation to open, disturbed, or early-successional habitats \citep{lohbeckSuccessionalChangesFunctional2013, jakovacRoleLanduseHistory2021, dentUnitingNicheDifferentiation2021, hordijkLandUseHistory2024, chazdonDriversBenefitsNatural2025}. Conversely, shade-tolerant species were expected to increase with forest cover, connectivity, and late successional forest, as these species rely on shaded and stable microhabitats typical of more continuous and mature forest patches \citep{hordijkLandUseHistory2024, santo-silvaNatureSeedlingAssemblages2013, huancanunezSeedrainsuccessionalFeedbacksWet2021}.

While the direction of these relationships was expected to be consistent across forest types, differences in overall seed richness and the relative proportion of dispersal modes and functional guilds were predicted. Wet forest was predicted to have higher seed richness due to the higher water availability and length of growing season \citep{hordijkLandUseHistory2024}. Furthermore, wet forest was predicted to maintain higher biotic dispersal and shade-tolerant species due to higher animal activity and denser canopy cover \citep{poorterComprehensiveFrameworkVegetation2024, hordijkLandUseHistory2024}, whereas dry forests was predicted to have more pioneer and generalist species and abiotic dispersal strategies due to more open canopies and seasonal water limitations \citep{souzadepaulaRoleSeedRain2023, hordijkLandUseHistory2024}.
